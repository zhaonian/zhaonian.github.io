Your listening room

Privacy policy.

Updated November 24, 2026

Lyrae is developed by Zhaonian Luan. This policy describes the information used by the iPhone and iPad app and this support website. Contact jerry@zluan.io for privacy questions or requests.

Lyrae was previously named Cadenza. Your account, saved recordings and subscription remain the same. Apple and Google sign-in are supported; Facebook sign-in is currently unavailable. Feature availability can vary by app version.

Your privacy at a glance.

  • Your generated music is saved on your device and privately in your cloud library.
  • Listening links let anyone with the link listen in a browser. Pieces you publish can appear in Listen and search, and your personal musical note is not shown on public pages.
  • Apple, Google or Facebook verifies your sign-in. Google’s music-generation service receives your musical description and settings when you compose, and RevenueCat manages subscription access.
  • Facebook uses Limited Login. Automatic app-event logging, product-improvement logging and advertiser-identifier collection are disabled.

Your device and cloud library.

Your saved audio, titles, musical notes, favorites, and library metadata are stored in the app’s local files. Your sign-in session and unfinished request identifier, musical draft, and acceptance state are kept in the iOS Keychain. Generated recordings and their titles, musical notes, styles, durations, and creation dates are also saved privately on Lyrae’s backend so you can restore your collection on another device. Favorites remain local. You can remove a device download while retaining the cloud copy. Device backups may include app files according to your Apple backup settings.

Lyrae does not request access to your microphone, contacts, photos, or precise location. Lyrae does not use your information for advertising tracking. Facebook sign-in uses Limited Login, with automatic app-event logging, product-improvement logging, and advertiser-identifier collection disabled.

When you sign in.

Sign-in is required to enter the app after the introduction. Your first sign-in creates your account automatically. Saved sessions let you return without signing in each time; signing out or a rejected session returns you to the account screen. Public listening links do not require an account.

You can choose Apple, Google, or Facebook when that option is available to your account. The provider verifies your identity and sends Lyrae a signed identity token. Apple and Google also provide a one-time authorization code. We validate these to create a Lyrae account and keep session credentials in the iOS Keychain. Use the same provider to return to the same collection; accounts are not automatically linked by email.

The backend stores a provider-specific account-identifier hash, a random Lyrae account identifier, session-token hashes, and encrypted credentials needed to revoke the provider connection on account deletion. For Apple and Google this is a refresh token; for Facebook it is the app-specific user identifier used with our server credentials. Apple is not asked for your name or email. Google’s basic sign-in and Facebook’s public-profile login may supply profile information in their sign-in response, but Lyrae does not persist your profile name, photo, or email in its account database or publish them on listening pages. The sign-in providers process login and device information under their own policies.

When you compose.

After you enable permission to send prompts, your musical description, mood, ensemble, tempo, and approximate duration are sent through Lyrae’s server to Google’s music-generation service. After the server accepts a request, generation can continue while the app is closed or disconnected. The cloud stores the finished audio, and the app downloads it when you return with a connection. Your chosen library title is not included in the generation prompt.

The server temporarily stores the full generation prompt in a durable job record until the job completes, fails, or expires. Queue messages contain only the request identifier. The server keeps request identifiers, prompt hashes, timestamps, completion status, and account allowance records to deliver recordings, recover interrupted downloads, and prevent duplicate requests. The cloud library stores your original musical note and chosen settings along with the recording. Complete cloud recordings remain until you delete the piece or account. Incomplete uploads are queued for cleanup after about ten minutes.

Google processes the prompt and output under its Gemini API terms and privacy policy. Unpaid API services may use inputs and outputs to improve Google’s products and may involve human review. Paid API services have different data-use terms. Avoid sensitive, confidential, or personal information in musical descriptions. Lyrae now uses a billing-enabled Google project. Under Google’s paid-service terms, prompts and generated output are not used to improve Google’s products. The stable-song integration requests that Google not store an interaction for later API retrieval; Google may retain data for abuse prevention and legal purposes under its terms. The app explains the active service before you send a musical description.

Your public profile.

You can choose a public display name, unique handle, short biography and profile artwork. These fields, and your published pieces, appear on your creator profile. Your sign-in name and email are not used to populate it. Private pieces and their musical prompts are not shown on public profiles.

Sharing your music.

Recordings start private. Choosing Create listening link makes a piece unlisted: accessible to people who have its link, without adding it to the community feed or search. Anyone who has that link can see its title, artwork, mood, ensemble, composer inspiration, and duration, and play its audio in a browser without an account or the app. Your musical note, account identifier, and sign-in profile are not shown. Link previews may display the title and artwork in the service where you share the URL. Listening pages ask search engines not to index them; possession of the link grants access.

Stop sharing disables future access through that link and removes the piece from public discovery if it was published. Deleting the cloud piece or account also disables its links. Previously saved or exported copies, and previews cached by another service, are outside Lyrae’s control. Exporting an audio file uses your device’s share sheet and the destination you choose.

Public discovery and legacy playlists.

The current app focuses on individual pieces and favorites. The following playlist details apply to records created in earlier beta versions.

Playlists store your titles, descriptions, track references, order, and visibility on the backend under your account. New playlists are private. Choosing Publish makes a piece or playlist appear in Listen and search, and accessible to anyone through its public browser link. A public playlist shows its title and description and only pieces their owners have already published. Private pieces in that playlist remain visible only to their owners. Your personal generation note and sign-in identity remain private.

Making a playlist private disables its public page and removes it from discovery; it does not change the individual pieces. Making a piece private removes it from discovery and disables its public and unlisted links. The app may briefly retain already-fetched metadata, and audio already delivered cannot be recalled. Deleting a playlist keeps its recordings. Playlist records, your publication entries, hidden-creator preferences, and reports you submitted are removed when you delete your account.

Search terms are sent to the backend to return matching published music or playlists; Lyrae does not maintain an account search-history table. Public playback streams on demand and does not automatically save another creator’s recording to your library. If you hide a creator, we store that preference to filter your feed; it remains associated with your account until removed or the account is deleted. If you report an item, we store your account identifier, the item reference, reason, and date for developer review until reviewed and removed or your account is deleted. Reports temporarily withdraw an item from public discovery and disable its public and unlisted links while it is reviewed. The owner retains private access; recordings are not deleted by a report. Reviewed restrictions can be appealed through support. Contact support for a content or copyright concern.

Screening shared content.

Before a piece is published or an unlisted listening link is created, its title is checked by Cloudflare-hosted Llama Guard. Titles and descriptions for legacy public playlists, and edits to already shared titles or descriptions, are checked in the same way. Private musical notes, generation prompts and account identifiers are not included. If the check fails or is unavailable, the new publication or shared edit is not applied. The server caches a hash of the submitted public text and its verdict for up to 30 days, without storing the submitted text in the screening cache. Reports are held for developer review; automated checks do not determine copyright ownership or resolve every audio or artwork concern.

Cover artwork and notifications.

Free and trial pieces use predefined cover art. Eligible paid pieces may use Cloudflare Workers AI (FLUX) to generate a cover from a predefined visual direction, the piece’s mood, and its ensemble. Covers are saved with the recording. Private musical notes, user-entered titles, sign-in profiles, and personal email addresses are not included in the image prompt. If image generation fails, a predefined image is used.

If you opt into completion notifications, Apple provides a device push token that Lyrae stores with your account to send generation-complete alerts. Notification permission is requested when generating, and is optional. You can change it in iOS Settings. Tokens are removed on sign-out or account deletion; invalid tokens are removed after Apple rejects them.

Subscriptions.

Apple processes payments. Lyrae uses RevenueCat to validate purchases and provide subscription access. RevenueCat receives a pseudonymous app user identifier, purchase and receipt information, subscription status, and technical information such as app and operating-system versions. An anonymous RevenueCat identifier may be created before you sign in; it is associated with your Lyrae identifier when you sign in. Lyrae does not receive your payment-card number.

See RevenueCat’s privacy policy and Apple’s privacy policy for their practices.

Hosting, security, and support.

Cloudflare hosts the Lyrae API, listening pages, library metadata, and private cloud audio. Network providers process IP addresses and request information to deliver and protect the service. The app server temporarily uses a hash of an IP address to limit sign-in and public listening requests. Our support website is hosted with GitHub Pages; we do not add analytics scripts, tracking cookies, or advertising to these pages.

If you email support, we receive your email address and the information you choose to include. We use it to respond and resolve the issue. Service providers may process information in countries other than your own, including the United States.

Retention and deletion.

Local recordings remain until you delete them or remove the app. Exported copies and device backups are under your control. Sessions expire after 30 days. Server account and allowance records remain while your account exists; temporary security records expire automatically. An anonymous generation ledger, containing request identifiers and timestamps without your account identifier or prompt, remains to track total service usage.

Use Settings → Delete account to remove the server account, sessions, generation records, cloud-library metadata, and listening links, and queue cloud audio for deletion. The server also requests revocation of the selected sign-in provider connection and RevenueCat customer deletion. If a provider is unavailable, a minimal cleanup record is retained and retried; encrypted provider credentials are erased after revocation, and the cleanup record is erased after provider cleanup finishes. Recreating an account may be temporarily unavailable while this finishes.

Account deletion leaves local recordings on the device and does not cancel a subscription. Cancel separately in Apple Account subscription settings. Apple and other providers may retain transaction or security records under their own policies and legal obligations. Contact support if you cannot access the in-app deletion control or need help with a data request.

Facebook data deletion.

If you still have an active session for an account created with Facebook, open Settings → Delete account, and confirm. This removes your Lyrae server data and requests removal of the Facebook app connection as described above. If you cannot sign in, email jerry@zluan.io with the subject “Lyrae Facebook data deletion”; we will help verify ownership and process the request. Never send a password or authentication token. Removing the connection in Facebook settings alone is separate from deleting your Lyrae account.

Your choices.

Composing requires your agreement when you first create music. Each new submission starts only when you choose to create a piece. Leaving the app does not withdraw a request already accepted in the cloud. You can sign out, remove downloads, delete cloud recordings, revoke listening links, export your music, manage your subscription, or request account deletion separately. Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct, delete, or restrict use of personal information. Contact us to make a request; we may need to verify account ownership.

Age and changes.

Lyrae’s cloud composition service is intended for adults aged 18 and over. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. Contact us if you believe a child has supplied personal information.

We may update this policy as the app changes. The date above identifies the latest revision; material changes to data handling will be disclosed in the app before the affected feature is used.
